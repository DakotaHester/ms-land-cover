\begin{abstract}
    Deep learning semantic segmentation methods have shown promising performance for very high \SI{1}{\meter} resolution land cover classification, but the challenge of collecting large volumes of representative training data creates a significant barrier to widespread adoption of such models for meter-scale land cover mapping over large areas.
    % Further, most very high spatial resolution imagery datasets contain relatively few spectral bands, making such models particularly reliant on spatial features to distinguish between classes, increasing the computational complexity of the models and the amount of training data required to achieve satisfactory performance.
    In this study, we present a novel label-efficient approach for statewide \SI{1}{\meter} land cover classification using only 1,000 annotated reference image patches.
    % The methodology integrates recent research on self-supervised learning by applying the ``Bootstrap Your Own Latent'' pre-training strategy using large amount of unlabeled color-infrared aerial images (377,921 256×256 \SI{1}{\meter} pixel patches) to pre-train a ResNet-101 convolutional encoder.
    The methodology integrates recent research on self-supervised learning, stratified sampling for representative samples, model ensembling, and test-time augmentation procedures. 
    The self-supervised learning uses “Bootstrap Your Own Latent” pre-training strategy using large amount of unlabeled color-infrared aerial images (377,921 256\(\times\)256 \SI{1}{\meter} pixel patches) to pre-train a ResNet-101 convolutional encoder.
    The learned encoder weights were subsequently transferred into multiple segmentation architectures (FCN, U-Net, Attention U-Net, DeepLabV3+, UPerNet, PAN), which were then fine-tuned using very small training dataset sizes using cross-validation (250, 500, 750 patches).
    Among the fine-tuned models, we obtained the 86.43\% overall accuracy and 74.04\% macro F1 score using an ensemble of the best performing U-Net models for comprehensive \SI{1}{\meter}, 8-class land cover mapping, covering over 123 billion pixels over the state of Mississippi, USA.
    Detailed qualitative and quantitative analysis revealed accurate mapping of open water, forested areas, and developed regions, while highlighting challenges in accurate delineation between cropland, herbaceous, and barren land cover types.
    These results show that self-supervised learning is an effective strategy for reducing the need for large volumes of manually annotated data, directly addressing a major limitation to high spatial resolution land cover mapping at scale.
\end{abstract}
